Centrifugal Pump Sizing Documentation

This tab summarizes the engineering checks represented by the workbook and common centrifugal-pump sizing practice. Workbook references include MET Pump Design, Pump Calcs, User Defined Units, PumpSelection, Pump Efficiency Data, Pipe_Table, and the PumpSelectionChart chart sheet.

Pump Suction Straight Run Pipe Size Requirements

Provide a hydraulically stable approach to the pump suction. Avoid elbows, tees, partially open valves, reducers that trap vapor, and other flow-disturbing components immediately at the suction nozzle. Suction piping should normally be at least the pump-nozzle size and selected for low velocity and acceptable friction loss. Use an eccentric reducer flat-on-top for horizontal liquid suction where vapor-pocket avoidance is required. The final straight-run requirement depends on pump type, nozzle geometry and vendor/HI guidance.

Centrifugal Pump Hydraulic Selection and Driver Sizing Criteria

Determine rated and maximum capacity, system TDH, NPSHA, fluid properties, and operating range. Select a pump whose duty lies in the preferred operating region near BEP while maintaining NPSH margin. Hydraulic power is ρgQH; brake power divides hydraulic power by pump efficiency, and driver input further accounts for driver efficiency and appropriate service margin.

Centrifugal Pump Type Selection Process

The workbook's selection data includes ASME/API horizontal end-suction, between-bearing double-suction, horizontal multistage, vertical multistage/turbine, sealless and low-flow/high-head configurations. Selection depends on flow, head, pressure, NPSH, fluid hazard, reliability, speed/specific speed, maintenance and applicable project standards.

Sealing Application Guide Based on Process Material Hazard

Seal technology should be screened against fluid hazard, volatility, toxicity, flammability, solids, temperature and leakage consequences. The workbook explicitly reports fluid hazard category, minimum mechanical-seal technology, flush-plan requirement, leak detection, and minimum sealless technology.

Criteria For Sealed API 610 / ISO 13709 Centrifugal Pumps

For services governed by API 610 / ISO 13709, confirm allowable pump type, operating region, pressure/temperature capability, nozzle loads, rotor dynamics, materials, mechanical seals, auxiliary systems, testing and driver requirements against the current project specification and applicable standard edition. This calculator does not replace vendor/API compliance review.

Pressure Drop Table For Conical Strainers

Temporary suction strainers add loss that can materially reduce NPSHA. Evaluate clean and expected fouled pressure drop at maximum flow and include that loss in suction hydraulics. The workbook includes a suction-strainer selection/check within the pipe-data section.

Temporary Conical Strainer - 200% Open Area - Class 150, 300 & 600 RF

For raised-face flanged temporary strainers, select dimensions compatible with nominal line size and flange class. A nominal 200% open-area criterion refers to perforated/screen flow area relative to pipe flow area; actual free area and pressure loss must be checked for the selected mesh/perforation and fouling allowance.

Temporary Conical Strainer - 200% Open Area - Class 150, 300 & 600 RTJ

RTJ installations require geometry compatible with the ring-joint flange arrangement and project piping details. Confirm gasket/ring seating, insertion dimensions, pressure class and removal plan. Hydraulic loss should be evaluated on the actual effective open area.

Hydraulic Institute 9.8 (1998) - Pump Intake Design

Intake design aims to deliver uniform, swirl-controlled, air-free flow to the pump. For sumps and wet wells, assess submergence, approach velocity, vortices, recirculation, spacing and intake geometry. Detailed intake acceptance should be checked against the applicable Hydraulic Institute guidance and project requirements.

Hydraulic Institute 20.3 (2010) - Rotodynamic Pump Efficiency Prediction

Pump efficiency varies with flow, pump type, size and specific speed. The workbook contains a dedicated Pump Efficiency Data sheet and curve-fit data for horizontal end-suction, multistage, vertical, slurry and double-suction categories. Use vendor curves when available; estimated efficiency is suitable for preliminary driver sizing, not final procurement.

Assumptions and Limitations

The browser calculation assumes steady, incompressible, single-phase liquid flow and Darcy-Weisbach piping losses. It does not reproduce every VBA UDF, economic line-sizing rule, seal decision tree, control-valve routine or Stage-3 vendor-curve fit embedded in the workbook. Final pump selection requires vendor curves and project/API/HI review.
