Shell stress screening—

This workbench uses simplified nozzle equations and a thin-shell stress screen. It is not certified valve sizing, a two-phase thermodynamic model, a code MAWP calculation, a flare-network model, or an atmospheric-tank vent calculation. Verify the relief scenario, fluid properties, applicable standards, manufacturer coefficients, and disposal system before design use.

Feed & relieving fluid properties

Cp and Cv are gas/vapor seed values. Liquid density and viscosity are reference seeds. No automatic temperature/pressure property correction is applied. Latent heat is a reference value; it can become invalid near critical conditions. Zero means unavailable and blocks a heat-load calculation.

Vessel geometry & wall

Cylindrical length is straight-shell length; volume uses that cylinder only. Wetted area includes the wetted shell and flat end/bottom surfaces. Heads and complex internals are not modeled. The atmospheric-tank option requires a separate venting method; pressure-relief area screening is suppressed.

Relief scenario & device

Gas calculation uses absolute relieving pressure, Cp/Cv and Z. Liquid calculation assumes nonflashing, incompressible flow with a user-entered viscosity correction. Mixed phase needs a qualified supplied mass flux. Feed equipment is chosen from feed phase, not the relieving phase.

Heat load & wetted area

Heat-load case: vapor generation = user heat input ÷ latent heat. Heat input is either entered directly or specified heat flux × geometric wetted area × factor. This is not an implemented API fire correlation; environmental factors and effective fire area remain engineering inputs.

Metallurgy & temperature-dependent allowable stress

Each material keeps its own editable table. Allowable stresses start blank: material name alone does not define product form, grade, heat treatment, code edition or allowable stress. Enter qualified values and their source. Interpolation uses bracketing points only; no extrapolation. Manufacturer tensile/yield properties are not automatically treated as design allowables.

Calculation basis & source links

Equations, limits and state handling

Relieving P₁ = atmospheric pressure + set gauge pressure × (1 + selected overpressure fraction); backpressure P₂ is atmospheric pressure for ATM, or atmosphere + entered flare-header gauge pressure for FLARE. Total P₂ includes the entered header pressure only; outlet pipe losses must be included by the user. A = mass load / corrected mass flux. Gas: isentropic critical/subcritical nozzle relation with constant Cp/Cv and a user Z approximation. Liquid: mass flux = √(2ρΔP). Mixed-phase flux is supplied by the user. No flashing, viscous-flow, gas-liquid equilibrium, reactive, decomposition, thermal-expansion or built-up-backpressure solver is included.

Pressure stress: cylindrical hoop stress = P D / (2 tₑ E); spherical membrane stress = P D / (4 tₑ E), with tₑ = nominal wall × (1 − mill tolerance) − corrosion allowance. This is an approximate thin-wall screen, only reported when D/tₑ ≥ 20. Allowable-stress interpolation uses the entered metal temperature; it is not a design-code thickness calculation. Metal mass = approximate geometric surface area × nominal thickness × metal density. Thermal capacity = mass × metal Cp. Blank stress values do not become zero.

All numerical state uses SI: K, Pa, m, kg/s, J/(kg·K), J/kg, Pa·s, m². Unit switching changes display only. Changing fluid reloads property seeds; changing metallurgy retains separate stress tables. Every input edit recalculates. Orifice areas are editable effective areas; catalog letter seeds require confirmation against the selected valve manufacturer.
