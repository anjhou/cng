Series Orifice Anti-Cavitation Design — Documentation

Readable HTML replacement. The workbook's Documentation sheet is stored as embedded graphics rather than selectable worksheet text. The readable instructions below are transcribed from the workbook's User Instructions content and retain its terminology and warnings.

Design and Rating Guidance

1. Design-mode convergence

After entering data for a new design, run the design convergence calculation to obtain converged results. Rating mode does not require this design convergence step.

2. Optimization using downstream back pressure

To help minimize the required number of orifice plates, the workbook recommends including downstream piping pressure drop as back pressure by adding it to the desired final downstream pressure. Static-head back pressure can also be increased by locating the plates below the discharge-vessel return nozzle where appropriate. The intent is to suppress flashing and subsequent cavitation.

Warning Notes

Design Acceptable

The design is acceptable when its operating point is to the left of the Limiting Boundary for Orifice Plate Designs and the difference between required total ΔP and design-provided total ΔP is less than 0.1%.

Backside taper required

If plate thickness divided by bore diameter exceeds 0.125, the workbook requires a tapered outlet on the backside of the orifice plate so the plate exhibits the intended flow characteristics and resistance. If one plate requires tapering, the workbook advises tapering all plates to reduce installation-error potential.

Design Unacceptable — cavitation boundary

If one or more plate operating points lie to the right of the limiting-boundary curve, the workbook directs the user to add plates and rerun the design calculation or obtain specialist assistance. The region between the Critical Cavitation Limit and the limiting boundary is intended only for limited cases where it changes the required plate count.

Design Unacceptable — convergence error

A difference greater than 0.1% between required and actual total ΔP indicates excessive convergence error. In rating mode this can indicate that the entered bore sizes do not provide the required total pressure drop. In design mode, verify that convergence was executed and that per-plate ΔP constraints are not preventing the required total ΔP from being achieved.

Minimum Required Plate Thickness

The workbook calculates a minimum allowable plate thickness from the maximum allowable stress of the selected plate material. Typical allowable-stress values versus temperature are provided on the Plate Allowable Stress sheet. The workbook notes that thicker plates may be used provided t/d < 0.125 is maintained; otherwise the outlet taper requirement applies.

High plate power dissipation

When plate power dissipation is excessive, the workbook recommends increasing the number of plates, reducing maximum ΔP per plate, or considering a multi-hole/perforated plate design. Its warning notes associate excessive dissipation with increased piping vibration and possible line damage.

Rating Mode — Data Mismatch

If required ΔP and actual ΔP do not agree, the workbook identifies a mismatch among flow rate, required differential pressure and entered orifice sizes. The original workbook provides a Solve Delta macro that varies design mass flow rate; alternatively an input orifice size can be adjusted.

Rating Mode — Unacceptable Plate Cavitation Expected

For rating cases, plate operating points to the right of the Limiting Boundary for Orifice Designs indicate unacceptable cavitation according to the workbook guidance. Use the Calculation-tab graph to identify the affected plate or plates.
