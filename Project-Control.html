Project Controls

Predictable project delivery through integrated scope, cost, schedule, progress, risk and quality management for U.S. Gulf Coast refinery projects.

Project Controls supports delivery of approved scope within budget and schedule, while tracking quality and safety requirements in coordination with accountable disciplines.

Engineering designs the project, Construction builds it, but Project Controls tells management whether the project will succeed or fail.

Five characteristics of effective USGC controls

01 · Single source of truth

One approved schedule, budget and progress system across Engineering, Procurement, Construction and Operations.

02 · Early warning

Forecast a 30-day delay six weeks ahead based on productivity, rather than merely reporting a historical delay.

03 · Tight change management

Control design changes, operations requests, turnaround additions and late engineering changes. Record cost, schedule, risk and approval for each.

04 · Operations integration

Track outages, turnarounds, tie-ins and startup windows. Missing a turnaround can delay completion by a year.

05 · Reliable progress measurement

Use documents completed, equipment shipped, pipe installed, steel erected and cable pulled rather than subjective opinions.

Nine-step project control process

01 · Establish project baseline

Approve scope, cost and schedule baselines.

02 · Develop WBS

Engineering, procurement, civil, structural, mechanical, piping, electrical, instrumentation and commissioning.

03 · Resource-loaded schedule

Activities, durations, logic links, resources and labor hours. Indicative refinery schedules: 2,000–50,000 activities depending on size.

04 · Estimate and control budget

Control estimates, cost codes, budget allocation and cash flow curves.

05 · Progress measurement system

Engineering deliverables, piping inches installed, structural tons erected, cable installed and instrumentation devices installed.

06 · Monitor progress

Typically weekly: earned value, cost variance, schedule variance and productivity.

07 · Forecast final outcome

Forecast final cost, completion date and remaining contingency.

08 · Manage changes

Identify scope change → technical review → cost impact → schedule impact → risk assessment → approval → controlled baseline update.

09 · Closeout and lessons learned

Cost reconciliation, final schedule analysis, contractor evaluations, benchmarking and lessons learned.
